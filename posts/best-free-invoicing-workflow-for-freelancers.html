Best Free Invoicing Workflow for Freelancers

Most small businesses don't have a invoicing problem. They have a "we never decided how this works" problem. The tooling is usually fine. The steps aren't.

A workflow that survives contact with a real week looks like this.

Write the steps down, in order

Not in your head. On paper. Every step someone has to remember is a step someone will eventually skip. Five to nine steps is the sweet spot — longer than that and the process is doing too much.

Pick one place for the information

A spreadsheet, a folder, a tool — one. Not two. Two is how the same number ends up meaning different things on different days.

Put a number on it

How long does the step take? How often does it fail? If you can't measure it, you can't tell whether changing it helped. Even a rough number beats a feeling.

Review weekly, not constantly

Fifteen minutes on a fixed day. Look at what broke. Change one thing. Constant fiddling is how a working process stops working.
